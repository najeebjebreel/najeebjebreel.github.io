About

Najeeb Jebreel is a Juan de la Cierva Postdoctoral Fellow in the KISON research group at the Universitat Oberta de Catalunya (UOC). His research is on privacy and security in machine learning: membership inference and privacy auditing, machine unlearning, differential privacy, and attacks and defences in federated learning.

Before joining UOC in July 2026, he was a postdoctoral researcher in the CRISES research group at Universitat Rovira i Virgili (URV), where he received his PhD in 2023 cum laude with the URV Extraordinary Doctorate Award. He is affiliated with the UNESCO Chair in Data Privacy at URV.

His work has appeared in PoPETs, ESORICS, IEEE TNNLS, Neural Networks, Knowledge-Based Systems and Artificial Intelligence Review, and has been cited more than 720 times (h-index 11, Google Scholar). His paper on layer-wise feature analysis against backdoor attacks received the Best Paper Award at PAKDD 2023 and was invited to IJCAI 2024.

He serves on the program committee of PoPETs 2027, has supervised three Master's theses and currently supervises two PhD students. He has collaborated with Huawei Technologies and the Notre Dame–IBM Technology Ethics Lab, and has contributed to projects funded by the European Commission, INCIBE and the Spanish State Research Agency.

Research

  • Privacy auditing and membership inference

    Membership inference attacks are the standard tool for measuring what a model leaks about its training data. I re-evaluate them under realistic conditions: models trained with ordinary anti-overfitting practice, decision thresholds calibrated without access to the target's data, skewed membership priors, and reproducibility across training runs. Under these conditions even the strongest attacks, such as LiRA, are much weaker and less reliable than usually reported. I also study why a small set of samples stays vulnerable in models that generalise well.

    Key papers: LiRA (PoPETs 2026), Critical review (ESORICS 2026), Beyond overfitting (DPM 2025)

  • Machine unlearning

    Data protection law gives people the right to have their data forgotten, including by models trained on it. I develop unlearning methods with formal guarantees: models are first trained on data protected with differential privacy or k-anonymity, so later forgetting requests can be served cheaply with guarantees inherited from the privacy model. I also study unlearning in large language models and how to check whether forgetting actually happened.

    Key papers: Survey (AI Review 2025), DP2Unlearning, EUPG, MIA defenses for unlearned data (MDAI 2025)

  • Security of federated and centralised learning

    Federated learning trains models without pooling raw data, but it opens new attack surfaces. I have built defences against label-flipping, Byzantine and backdoor attacks, proposed fragmented federated learning to improve privacy and security together, and introduced a layer-wise feature analysis for detecting backdoors that received the PAKDD 2023 Best Paper Award. Earlier work covers watermarking deep learning models to prove ownership.

    Key papers: LFighter, FL-Defender, Fragmented FL (IEEE TNNLS), Layer-wise feature analysis (PAKDD 2023), KeyNet
